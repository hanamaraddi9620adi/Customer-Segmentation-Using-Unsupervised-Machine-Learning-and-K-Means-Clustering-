# Abstract
## Customer Segmentation Using Unsupervised Learning on Wholesale Purchasing Data

This project explores customer segmentation using the uploaded Wholesale Customers dataset, containing 440 observations and six product-category spending variables. The objective is to identify purchasing patterns without predefined customer labels. Data validation found no missing values or duplicate rows. Extreme spending observations were retained because they may represent legitimate customer behavior. The six spending variables were transformed using log(1 + x) and standardized, while Channel and Region were excluded from model training and retained for interpretation. K-Means and Ward hierarchical clustering were compared for two through eight clusters using silhouette, Davies-Bouldin and Calinski-Harabasz measures. A silhouette-based selection rule with a minimum cluster-size constraint selected K-Means with two clusters of 252 and 188 customers and a silhouette score of 0.2903. Median profiles distinguish a group with higher fresh and frozen spending from a group with higher grocery, milk and detergent spending. Twenty subsampling runs produced a mean Adjusted Rand Index of 0.9649 relative to the full-data assignments. However, modest cluster separation and strong sensitivity to preprocessing limit definitive interpretation. PCA is used for visualization only. The study delivers a reproducible exploratory segmentation workflow and customer profiles that could inform future product-bundling and inventory studies, subject to business validation.

Dataset reference: UCI Wholesale Customers, https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/292/wholesale+customers , DOI 10.24432/C5030X.
